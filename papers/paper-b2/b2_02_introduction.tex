\section{Introduction}
Paper~0 defines the Invariance-Based Architectural Investigation Protocol v1.0 as a reproducible scientific instrument for investigating candidate architectural invariants. Paper~B1 applied Protocol v1.0 to an initial three-system pilot cohort. Paper~B2 applies the same frozen protocol to a governance/authorization cohort to evaluate whether an explicit Reasoning\,$\rightarrow$\,Legitimacy\,$\rightarrow$\,Execution (RLE) boundary is observed under the protocol's operationalization.

Paper~B2 treats all new evidence as belonging to this study. Paper~B1 remains unchanged; any cross-paper comparison is interpretive only and must be grounded in protocol outputs rather than post-hoc protocol modification.

\subsection{Research question (cohort-bounded)}
Under what conditions does an RLE boundary appear in governance/authorization systems, as operationalized by Protocol v1.0 and evaluated over the Paper~B2 cohort?

\subsection{Scope and non-goals}
The investigation is limited to the evaluated cohort and to the evidence representations admitted under the B2 protocol registration (Section~\ref{sec:protocol-registration}). The paper does not attempt to:
(i) claim that an observed boundary is universal,
(ii) derive a formal proof (Program~A), or
(iii) retroactively alter Protocol v1.0.

\subsection{Contributions (protocol-defined and cohort-bounded)}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A Protocol v1.0 execution over a governance/authorization cohort using a frozen instrument.
  \item A synchronized BOR/SRF/DER/MSR artifact chain and canonical Comparative Dataset for all nine cohort systems, with Analysis, Retained Classification, and Cohort Conclusion complete.
  \item A lifecycle status record that preserves the boundary between completed Comparative Dataset construction and the complete Analysis, Retained Classification, and Cohort Conclusion stages.
\end{enumerate}
